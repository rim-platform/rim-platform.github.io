Navigation and window width

Every place in the app can be reached from a visible control or a command, at every window width. Commands run from the palette and from a keybind. The commands this app adds itself, such as the ones for the audit log and the panels, are not on the server's list, so macros and the AI agent do not get them.

Window widths #

The shell measures its own width and sorts it into one of three classes. The person's saved choices, such as the hidden conversation list and zen mode, are never changed by the class.

Class Width What changes
wide 1100 px and up Nothing: the workspace rail and the tool panel sit beside the conversation, under the top bar, and a thread, the pinned messages and a plugin panel take their own columns.
medium 760 to 1099 px The side panel stays. A thread, the pinned messages and a plugin panel in the tool panel slot open over the right of the main area, up to 420 px wide, full height. Escape closes the one in front, and focus returns to where it was.
narrow under 760 px The workspace rail and tool panel are hidden. The top bar stays, 48 px high: the menu button at its left opens them as a drawer over the main area, then the search field, Plugins and Notifications. Below 400 px the search field says only "Search" and its accessible name stays "Search or run a command". Back and Forward are hidden. A press outside the drawer, Escape, or choosing a conversation, a page or a plugin panel closes it. A thread, the pinned messages and a plugin panel fill the main area. Settings lists its sections in one menu above the content.

The drawer holds the same workspace rail (Home, direct messages, the workspaces, audit log, settings, account menu) and conversation list as the wide layout. When a member list or a new-message or new-channel dialog is asked for in a narrow window, the drawer opens to show it. The notification inbox opens under the top bar at every width.

The top bar #

The top bar sits above the conversation list and the main area on every page, and zen mode hides it with the rails. It is a landmark named "Top bar"; Tab reaches it, and so does moving between panes (workspace.navigate_panel_left and workspace.navigate_panel_right), between the workspace rail and the conversation list. On the left are Back and Forward. In the middle is a button that looks like a search field and runs workspace.open_search, which opens the search box under it (Search UI). It names what it searches: the open workspace, the direct messages in the direct space, or everything on Home and Unreads, and it shows the keystroke bound to workspace.open_search. On the right, Plugins opens the menu of plugin panels under it and shows only while a running plugin has a panel, and Notifications opens the notification inbox under it. Escape closes either one and gives focus back to its button.

Back and Forward (workspace.go_back, workspace.go_forward) move through the places opened in this window: a workspace, a page, and the conversation open on it. The history keeps the newest 50, and opening a new place after Back drops the ones that were forward. It is the app's own: the browser's history is never changed, so the web app's own back button still leaves the page as before. Neither command has a default keystroke, since Alt+ArrowLeft is Back to Workspace; bind them in Settings > Keybinds. The mouse's back and forward buttons are not used.

In the desktop app, which draws its own window frame, the top bar is the title bar: its empty areas drag the window, and the window controls sit at its far right outside macOS. Zen mode, the sign-in screens and the workspace picker keep the plain title bar.

These are narrow windows. The web app has no phone layout: there are no gestures and no bottom bar.
