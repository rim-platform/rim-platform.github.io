The relay

A relay is the server a person's app talks to. It keeps their account, devices and profile card, queues what other servers send them, files the conversations their DM settings take as requests in a request inbox, and forwards their requests to the servers that host the spaces they use. Every server is the relay of its local users unless it runs with RIM_ROLE=host. This page explains what a relay and a host do, for operators and for client authors. The contract is docs/specs/server-to-server-v1.md; the server's key, id and transport settings are in docs/server-identity.md; deploying a public relay is deploy/README.md and running one is docs/operations/relay-public.md.

Nothing here runs until server-to-server traffic is on (RIM_PUBLIC_BASE_URL is an https URL). Before that, a server is the relay and host of its own users only, as it always was.

The direct space #

Each server has one server row of kind direct, named "Direct messages", created at start. It holds the DMs and groups its users start with people on other servers, and is never listed by servers nor found by name. Everyone in it is a guest: nobody sees its member list, and nobody creates channels, invites or plugins there. Local users join it on first use: a signed-in relayInfo, or any read or write of the direct space that needs membership; people from other servers join when a local user adds them. Custom emojis, plugins, workspace keybinds, pipelines, workspace state and channels answer VALIDATION ("not available in the direct space") there, reads and writes alike, and the refused call joins nobody.

A conversation's home is the relay whose direct space holds it. The home sequences it: it stores the messages, Commits and Welcomes, and every member's app reads and writes there, through their own relay when it is elsewhere.

People from other servers #

When a local user adds someone from another server, this server fetches their identity (key log, devices with certificates and revocations, profile card) from their relay, checks every signature, and stores them as a user row of kind remote named r_ and the first 30 characters of their principal id. Their relay pushes identity changes (POST /s2s/v1/identity) to the servers that queued items or filed requests for them; principalDevices also refetches a remote person's identity when the stored copy is over an hour old. If such a person later signs up here with their key, the remote row becomes their local account and keeps its memberships.

Remote users never sign in here and never run account mutations here: sign-in, sign-up, device and key changes, card publishing, profile, workspace creation, device links, instance invites and the relay's own mutations are refused with FORBIDDEN ("not available to remote members").

Queues and the outbox #

Every event of a space with remote members becomes a signed host event (host-event) on a stream: c:<conversationId> for a conversation's events, u:<principalId> for one person's (their notifications, mentions, read states, Welcomes, DM invites, and for a guest the directory events about them), w for other space-wide events, e for typing and presence. Each stream's seq counts from 1 without gaps; e has none. The host appends one outbox row per relay that should get the event (for a workspace, the relays holding an active delegation of the person; for the direct space, the relays the person's newest card lists, else the relay that served their identity) and a sender delivers each relay's rows in order (POST /s2s/v1/deliver), backing off from 1 second to 5 minutes. Outbox rows are kept 7 days. A relay that refuses a person as unknown is not sent their events for 24 hours. Each server process keeps that refusal in memory: a restart forgets it, and with several nodes each node learns it from the relay on its own.

A relay stores each envelope once and one item per recipient. Each device reads its user's items above its cursor; a device added later starts at the newest item queued before it. An item is deleted once every non-revoked device of its user has acknowledged it, and after the retention period regardless. Over the per-user byte cap the oldest items go first and the devices online get a QueueOverflow notice; a device offline at the time sees the gap in the stream's seq. A relay pulls what a host still holds for it (GET /s2s/v1/outbox) every minute, so a delivery missed while the relay was down arrives on the next pull.

The request inbox #

A relay files a conversation's invite (DmInvite) by the recipient's effective DM setting, the three layers of docs/messaging.md ("Who May Start A DM"): the operator's RIM_DM_INBOUND, then the floors of the recipient's workspaces, then the recipient's choice, with fellow members of a workspace hosted here judged by the recipient's choice for that workspace (or its default) and delivered when they are its Owner or a Moderator. What the setting delivers is recorded as accepted at once; what it takes as a request is filed as a DM request; what it refuses is answered blocked. A request keeps the sender's card and holds up to three items of the conversation; later ones are dropped until it is accepted. Items of a conversation the relay has no invite for are refused (not_subscribed), so a host cannot skip the inbox. A decided conversation keeps its decision when the settings change later.

The same rule covers conversations of this server's own direct space whose creator is someone else (a person from another relay creating a DM with a local user through the forward path, or a local user), and DMs between members of a workspace this server hosts. The server files its own invite for the local member; until they accept, the conversation is left out of their inboxConversations, its events out of their workspaceEvents, and its notifications, mentions and unread counts out of the notification list, the bell, unreadCounts and the email digest; its first events are held with the request. Everything appears once they accept. This needs server-to-server traffic to be on; with it off, conversations in the direct space are live at once.

Action Effect
acceptDmRequest Held items released; later items flow; the sender becomes a contact
ignoreDmRequest Held items deleted but the invite, which stays with the request; later items dropped; the request expires after 30 days
blockPrincipal Pending requests from the principal ignored as above; its invites (blocked) and key package claims refused
reportDmRequest Filed in relay_abuse_report for the operator and audited as relay.dm_request_reported; a pending request is ignored
Nothing Expires after the retention period

Limits: 100 pending requests per recipient and RIM_RELAY_DM_REQUESTS_PER_DAY (20) per sender principal per day on this relay; past them invites are refused as limited. A claim of a local user's key packages by a stranger (not a contact, never accepted, sharing no workspace here) gets last-resort packages only, at most 20 claims per stranger per day.

With RIM_DM_INBOUND=partners the identity endpoint (GET /s2s/v1/principals/<id>) answers only the servers RIM_DM_PARTNERS lists, and with none no server; everyone else gets 404 as for an unknown principal, so a leaked directory reaches nobody. Key package claims from those servers are refused (403). A server where the person holds an active subscription to a workspace is answered either way: they joined it. Outbound, RIM_DM_OUTBOUND decides which servers a local user may start a DM with, claim key packages from, and have direct-space events sent to (docs/messaging.md).

Forwarded requests #

A local user's app reaches a space hosted elsewhere at /api/remote/<hostServerId>/<path> with its session cookie (and X-CSRF-Token for POST), a device-signed request statement in Rim-Device-Request (unpadded base64url of the payload) and its signature in Rim-Device-Signature. The relay checks that the statement names the session's principal and device, that the device is not revoked and that the signature verifies, then forwards the body unchanged to <host>/s2s/v1/forward/<path> and returns the host's answer unchanged. A host it cannot reach is 502 {"error":"upstream_unavailable","hostServerId":…}.

The host checks the S2S signature, the device request (a device of a user it holds, its own id, the method, path and body hash, the seq window), and that the relay may carry the user's traffic: the relay holds an active delegation of that device for a workspace the user is still a member of, or the user is a member of its direct space and their newest card lists the relay's origin (403 not_delegated otherwise). It then runs GraphQL (POST /api), attachment downloads and uploads, avatar uploads (POST /api/files/avatar), stored-file downloads (GET /api/files/<path>) or plugin bundle downloads as that user and device, with no session, and signs every answer, refusals included, with Rim-Host-Response and Rim-Host-Signature. Per-IP limits key on relay:<serverId>:<principalId>.

A device request's seq is accepted once: the host remembers accepted values for at least 10 minutes and until they fall below its window (5 minutes in microseconds under the device's highest), and refuses a repeat or a lower value with 409 {"error":"replay","highestSeq":…}.

Workspaces with members from other servers #

A person whose relay is another server joins a workspace here through that relay. Their app gets a login challenge from this server (POST /s2s/v1/join/challenge, 120 seconds, single use), with the workspace its invite names, and signs it with this server's id as the audience, so the proof is good here only. It also signs a delegation: its relay may receive this workspace's events for the person and forward that device's signed requests here, for at most 24 hours. The relay presents both with the person's key log, devices and card (POST /s2s/v1/join). This server checks everything, joins the person by the invite exactly as joinServerByInvite does (bans included; a current member sends no invite), stores them as a remote user and keeps the delegation.

A delegation is accepted when its device is a live device of the person, it names this server and the presenting relay, its relay key hash names a key of the relay's log that no recovery rotation replaced, the person is a current, unbanned member, and it runs at most 24 hours. It is kept until its expiry or a day after acceptance, whichever is first, one per workspace, device and relay. Relays renew them (POST /s2s/v1/subscriptions, at most 64 at a time, each accepted or refused with a reason) and cancel them (POST /s2s/v1/subscriptions/cancel). A workspace's events go to the relays holding an active delegation of a recipient; those relays may forward the device's requests. A kick or a ban ends the person's delegations once their MemberLeft is queued; a revoked device's delegations end with it.

Relays report whether their users are online (POST /s2s/v1/presence); this server shows a remote member online in every workspace their relay holds a delegation for, until the relay reports them offline.

A revocation of a remote member's device arrives from their relay (POST /s2s/v1/revocations) or straight from their app (applyRemoteRevocation). This server checks it against its copy of the person's key log, marks the device revoked, ends its delegations and sessions, removes it from encrypted groups, and answers a signed acknowledgement; a repeat gets the same one.

When their relay is down, a remote member's desktop app may sign in here directly with its device key (loginWithKey, audience this server's id). This server first tries to refresh the person's identity from their relays, waiting up to 10 seconds, and issues a session of 24 hours. Account mutations stay refused on it.

Cleanup and audit #

Every minute the server deletes acknowledged and expired items, envelopes no item uses, expired requests, outbox rows over 7 days and accepted seq values out of the window, ends expired delegations and those of revoked devices or departed members, and marks this relay's expired subscriptions lapsed. Every 5 seconds it sends revocations due for another attempt. Audit events (target audit, ids only): relay.principal_blocked (scope user for a user's block, server for an operator's), relay.principal_unblocked, relay.blocked_principal_refused, relay.dm_request_reported, relay.abuse_report_filed, relay.abuse_report_resolved, blocked_email_refused (action signup or login), relay.registration_pow, relay.left, key_identity_adopted and host.revocation_applied; host.remote_join and host.delegation_accepted are also written to command_audit_log (initiator s2s).

The public relay #

A relay anyone may sign up on runs RIM_ROLE=relay with the settings of deploy/compose.relay.yml on top of deploy/compose.yml. This section says how its registration and blocks behave; docs/operations/relay-public.md has its quotas, the operator's commands and the abuse policy.

Registration. RIM_REGISTRATION_POW_BITS (1 to 40; 24 on the public relay) turns on pow registration, whatever RIM_OPEN_REGISTRATION says. signupWithKey then takes a registration token (instance invite or workspace invite) or proofOfWork: an 8-byte nonce, unpadded base64url, such that

SHA-256("rim.pow.v1\0" || this server's id || "\0" || inception payload || nonce)

starts with that many zero bits. The work is bound to this server and one principal, and checking it is one hash. Proof-of-work signups also count against the open-signup limit per IP and are audited as relay.registration_pow. Password signups take a token, as in invite-only mode. The descriptor says "registration":{"mode":"pow","powBits":24} and registrationMode answers POW. A signup link from rim_server invite create lets a person in without the work; it needs no account or workspace, so it works with RIM_ROLE=relay.

Quotas per local user are the RIM_RELAY_* settings above. The per-minute and per-day counters are in memory, per node.

Blocking a principal. rim_server principal block <principalId> --reason <text> stores the block in relay_principal_block, ends the sessions of the account holding the principal here and ignores its pending DM requests for every recipient. From then on, until principal unblock: its logins, signups and enrollments are refused (FORBIDDEN); deliveries to it are refused as unknown_principal and its invites as blocked; its key packages are not handed out, and it claims none; GET /s2s/v1/principals/<id> answers 404; forwarded requests it signs are refused with 403. principal list shows the blocks.

In the same transaction the command revokes the account's instance and workspace invites that still have uses left, adds its email (if it has one) to blocked_email, which refuses that address at signup (VALIDATION, "this email cannot sign up here") and at password login (FORBIDDEN), and records the reason as an abuse report, resolved by the block. Every account records the invite it signed up with and that invite's creator, so the command then lists the accounts the blocked one's invites created; --cascade blocks them too, recursively, each with the reason followed by "(invited by <username>)". An account without a key identity is blocked by its email alone. principal show <principalId> prints the account, its inviter, the tree of accounts its invites created, its invites, its open reports and its block. principal unblock lifts the principal's block and its email's, not the sessions or invites that ended.

Blocking a peer. rim_server peer block <serverId> sets the pinned peer's status to blocked, which the request check reads on every request: its requests get 403 peer_not_allowed and nothing is sent to it. peer unblock makes it active again. A forked peer stays forked; a peer never pinned is refused with RIM_S2S_DENIED_PEERS instead. Audited as s2s.peer_blocked and s2s.peer_unblocked.

Abuse reports. reportDmRequest in the app and reports that come by mail (rim_server abuse-reports add) both end in relay_abuse_report, which abuse-reports list and abuse-reports resolve work through.

For clients #

  • relayInfo { serverId roles directSpaceId } (no session needed) names the direct space.
  • remoteServer(origin) pins another server through the relay and returns its discovery document to verify and pin in the app.
  • remoteIdentity(principalId, relayUrl) returns a principal's key log, devices and card, from this server or fetched from relayUrl and cached for 24 hours. Verify the log against the principal id, each certificate against the log and the card against the head.
  • contacts, addContact(principalId, relayUrls), removeContact(principalId): the contact list the request inbox filters by. Relay URLs are 1 to 8 of 1 to 256 bytes of printable ASCII without ?, # or @. The caller's own principal is refused (VALIDATION, "cannot add yourself as a contact").
  • createConversation(input: { serverId: <directSpaceId>, remoteMembers: [{ principalId, relayUrls }], encrypted }) starts a DM (one remote member, no otherUserId or memberIds) or a group with people on other servers; people on this relay may be named the same way. A conversation with someone on another relay must be encrypted (otherwise VALIDATION, "conversations with people on other servers are encrypted"), and every member needs an MLS-active device. Any memberIds, or more than one remote member, makes a group. The caller needs a published profile card: their relays show it in the request inbox. Remote members that name nobody but the caller are refused (VALIDATION, "cannot create DM with yourself"). predecessor: { hostServerId, conversationId } records the conversation a successor replaces, and Conversation.predecessor returns it.
  • On a relay in pow registration mode (registrationMode is POW; the difficulty is the discovery document's registration.powBits), signupWithKey takes proofOfWork: count from 0, hash as in "The public relay" with the counter as the 8-byte big-endian nonce, and send the first nonce that meets the difficulty, unpadded base64url. A registration token instead skips the work.
  • dmRequests(status), acceptDmRequest, ignoreDmRequest, blockPrincipal, unblockPrincipal, reportDmRequest work the request inbox. A request's heldItems are host-signed envelopes to read before deciding, the DmInvite first; verify it to bind the conversation's space to its home before reading or leaving it.
  • relayStream(after) on the existing WebSocket yields the session device's items above after (its cursor when omitted), then live ones, as JSON strings: {"type":"RelayItem","id","hostServerId","workspaceId","envelope":{"payload","signature"}}, plus {"type":"DmRequestReceived","requestId"} and {"type":"QueueOverflow","spaces":[{"hostServerId","workspaceId"}]}. relayItems(after, limit) pulls the same items; ackRelayItems(upTo) moves the device's cursor. The session must be bound to a device.
  • For every item, check the envelope's signature under the host key pinned for hostServerId at its keySeq, that host is that server, and that the stream matches the event (c: its conversation, u: your principal). Apply a stream's events in seq order; on a gap, reconcile through the host (its change feed and streamHeads(serverId, streams) through the forward path).
  • leaveRelay cancels the caller's subscriptions at every host, then deletes their queue, requests, contacts and blocks on this relay.

Joining a workspace on another host #

  1. Read the invite link rim-invite:1:<hostServerId>:<hostKeyHash>:<inviteToken>:<hostOrigin> and pin the host with remoteServer(origin).

  2. remoteJoinChallenge(hostServerId, hostOrigin, inviteToken) returns { challengeId nonce expiresAt workspaceId }: workspaceId is the workspace the invite names (the invite is looked up, not used), null without one. Sign a login statement over the challenge with audience = the host's server id.

  3. Sign a delegation with the device key over rim.delegation.v1\0 || payload:

    {"v":2,"type":"delegation","principalId":"rimp1_…","deviceId":"<uuid>","relay":"<relay server id>",
 "relayKeyHash":"<hash of the relay's current key>","host":"<host server id>","workspaceId":"<uuid>",
 "scope":"workspace","nonce":"<16 random bytes, base64url>","issuedAt":"…","expiresAt":"…"}

    expiresAt is at most 24 hours after issuedAt. relayInfo names the relay; its key hash is in the relay's discovery document.

  4. joinRemoteWorkspace(input: { hostServerId, hostOrigin, inviteToken, proof, delegation }) returns { hostServerId workspaceId userId role }; inviteToken is null for a current member. The workspace is then used through /api/remote/<hostServerId>/api, and its events arrive on relayStream.

Keeping delegations fresh #

  • remoteSubscriptions lists { hostServerId workspaceId status expiresAt } with status active, lapsed (every delegation expired) or ended (cancelled).
  • When the current delegations have less than 6 hours left, an online device signs fresh ones and sends them with renewDelegations(delegations). Each entry of the answer is active with its expiresAt, or refused with a reason: one of the host's (not_member, expired, …), or host_unreachable (try again later; the subscription is unchanged) or host_refused (the host turned the presentation down). The relay logs what lies behind the last two.
  • While a device has relayStream open, the hosts see the person online; 30 seconds after the last stream closes they see them offline.

Revoking a device #

revokeDeviceKey on the relay also sends the revocation to every host and DM home holding the person, retrying at 10 s, 30 s, 1 min, 2 min, 5 min and then every 15 minutes for a day. deviceRevocationStatus(deviceId) lists { hostServerId status attempts ack } per host, status PENDING, ACKED or GAVE_UP; relayStream pushes {"type":"RevocationAcked","deviceId","hostServerId","ack":{"payload","signature"}} as each host acknowledges. Check each ack (a revocation-ack statement) under the host's pinned key and its revocationSha256 against the revocation. A host that has not acknowledged within 2 minutes gets the revocation from the app directly: applyRemoteRevocation(revocation) on the host's own /api needs no session and no CSRF header and returns the acknowledgement; an address may try it 20 times in 15 minutes.

Signing in to a host directly #

When the relay is unreachable, the desktop app calls keyLoginChallenge and loginWithKey on the host's own /api with audience = the host's server id. The session lasts 24 hours; account mutations are refused on it (FORBIDDEN), as through the relay.
