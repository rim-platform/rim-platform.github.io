End-to-end encryption with MLS v1

End-to-end encryption with MLS v1version 1status: implemented, normativerim-server/docs/specs/e2ee-mls-v1.mdcommit 64c6dea

Status: implemented, normative. Read key-identity-v1.md (keys, tagged encodings, statements) and device-linking-v1.md first. This document is the contract between the server (E2eeService), the rim_mls crate in rim-ui, the desktop client and the web client. Labels of the form S4-n below name the change that built an item: S4-1 the server, S4-2 the rim_mls crate, S4-3 to S4-5 the desktop client, S4-6 the web client.

Summary #

  1. DMs and groups are encrypted; channels, private ones included, stay plaintext. New DMs and groups are encrypted when their creator is ready; members who are not ready yet are invited and become members when they accept with a ready device. Existing ones are upgraded once, by a member device, behind a visible "encrypted since" marker. Old plaintext stays.
  2. One MLS group per conversation, suite 0x004F (X25519 + ML-KEM-768, AES-128-GCM, Ed25519). Members are devices. The server owns conversation membership; the group follows it.
  3. Credentials: a per-device MLS signature key certified by the device key (an mls-key statement), chained to the key log. Clients verify every leaf.
  4. Each device keeps 20 one-time key packages and one last-resort package on the server, 90-day lifetime. The server validates them on upload and hands them out once.
  5. The server is the sequencer: one Commit per (conversation, incarnation, epoch), enforced by a row lock and a unique index; the loser gets STALE_EPOCH. Application messages are accepted only in the current epoch, so the change feed is in epoch order.
  6. Every MLS message is a PrivateMessage. Its cleartext authenticated data carries what the server may read: mentions, the franking commitment, and the membership summary of a Commit. The server checks the summary against the members and the key packages it issued.
  7. An encrypted message is a message row with text null and an MLS envelope. Edits replace the envelope (same row, new version); deletes are server tombstones; reactions, pins, threads and forward origins are server metadata.
  8. A client decrypts each message once, in one transaction with storing its plaintext. A new device joins through a Welcome and reads older history from the drive archive.
  9. Attachments: AES-256-GCM under a per-file secret, uploaded as opaque blobs checked by SHA-256.
  10. Franking: an HMAC commitment in the authenticated data, a server receipt over it, and a report that reveals one message's content and franking key to the moderators.

Scope #

Conversation Encrypted in v1 Why
Direct Yes Two members, no shared history for later joiners, nothing server-side depends on its text
Group Yes Member-only by construction (is_private is always false for groups; the server has no other reader)
Channel, private No Channels are where server search, pipelines, bots and the agent work, and where later joiners read full history; MLS gives a new member nothing from before it joined. Encrypted private channels need history sharing and are deferred
Channel, public No Readable by every workspace member by design
  • Ready member. A user whose principal_id is set and who has at least one MLS-active device: not revoked, with a key package uploaded in the last 60 days (device_key.last_key_package_upload_at, set by every upload; key package rows themselves expire after 90 days), shown as PrincipalDevice.mlsActive. Bots, the CLI and password-only accounts are never ready.
  • Pending member (invitation). A member of an encrypted conversation who was not ready when they were added (Conversation.pendingMemberIds; not in memberIds or memberCount). They see the conversation in their inbox, with mlsGroup null, but cannot read, send or be notified there (FORBIDDEN, like a non-member), and their devices are not desired. The server never makes them a member on its own: a key-package upload changes nothing. They call acceptConversationInvite, which needs them ready (VALIDATION otherwise) and emits MemberAddedToConversation and MlsMembershipChanged; a member device then adds their devices like any pending add, and they read nothing from before. declineConversationInvite deletes the invitation. An invitation expires after 30 days, and one user holds at most RIM_LIMIT_PENDING_INVITES_PER_USER (default 50). Remote users and members of the relay's direct space are never invited: they must be ready.
  • An encrypted conversation has conversation.encrypted_at set. It is set when an encrypted conversation is created (createConversation(input: { encrypted: true })) or when the first Commit of an existing plaintext DM or group is accepted ("Migration"). It is never cleared.
  • Clients pin it. A device that holds group state for a conversation, or ever sees its encryptedAt or an encrypted entry in its feed, keeps the conversation encrypted on that device for good, through leaving it and NO_ACCESS too. When the server answers encryptedAt: null for a pinned conversation, the device shows "Encryption was reset by the server" and stops sending there: it never sends plaintext into it (sendMessage, replyToMessage, editMessage, forwardMessage, a plugin's post) and never upgrades it again.
  • In an encrypted conversation the server refuses plaintext writes (sendMessage, replyToMessage, editMessage on an encrypted row, forwardMessage into it: VALIDATION, this conversation is encrypted). createConversation(encrypted: true) refuses a creator who is not ready (VALIDATION, every member of an encrypted conversation needs a device set up for encryption), and adds invites every other member who is not ready; addConversationMembers invites them too.
  • Group devices: the devices that are leaves of the current MLS group, as the server records them from accepted Commits (mls_group_device). Desired devices: the MLS-active devices of the conversation's current members. Pending adds are desired devices not in the group; pending removes are group devices no longer desired (member left, removed, kicked or banned; device revoked; inactive for 60 days).
  • At most 100 members and 512 devices per encrypted conversation (VALIDATION beyond); pending members count.

Identity and credentials #

The mls-key statement #

A statement in the key-identity-v1.md sense (exact signed text, version 2 tagged keys, unknown and duplicate fields refused), signed by the device key over "rim.mls-key.v1\0" || payload, payload at most 16 KiB:

{"v":2,"type":"mls-key","principalId":"rimp1_…","deviceId":"<uuid>","mlsKey":"ed25519:…","issuedAt":"…"}
  • mlsKey is the device's MLS signature key: Ed25519 (the suite's signature scheme), generated by rim_mls, kept with the group state (never in the archive). One per device; a device that loses it generates a new key and statement and is re-added ("Lost state").
  • The MLS leaf's credential is a basic credential whose identity is the UTF-8 JSON {"payload":"<payload text>","signature":"<tagged signature>"} (appendix A.2); the leaf's signature_key must equal the raw bytes of mlsKey.
  • rim_identity gains the statement type and its verifier; servers and clients use the same code.

Verifying a leaf #

A client accepts a leaf (in a Welcome's tree, an Add, or a re-added leaf) only when all hold:

  1. identity parses as above and the payload is a valid mls-key statement.
  2. keyLog(principalId) replays (key-identity-v1.md) and contains the head this device pinned for the principal ("Key log head pinning" there), and principalDevices lists deviceId with a certificate that verifies against that log at its keySeq, no recovery rotation following that keySeq, and no revocation. A revocation counts when its signature verifies (by an identity key of the log, or by the revoked device's own key); a device this device once saw revoked (a verified revocation, or revokedAt) stays revoked on it whatever principalDevices answers later. A revocation that does not verify keeps the device out of that check only.
  3. The mls-key signature verifies under that certificate's deviceKey, and its principalId and deviceId equal the certificate's.
  4. mlsKey equals the leaf's signature_key.
  5. The principal is a current member of the conversation (from the server's member list, mapped by User.principalId; in a direct message, one of its two pinned principals, "Direct messages"), and no other leaf of the group has the same (principalId, deviceId).

Clients cache verified (principalId, deviceId, mlsKey) triples and recheck the device list when a MlsMembershipChanged event arrives. The server checks rules 1, 3 and 4 at key package upload, against the uploading session's own device.

A leaf that does not verify #

The client still processes the Commit or Welcome (refusing it would fork the group), then: refetches the key log and device list once; if the leaf still fails, it marks the conversation "unverified device present" naming the principal and device id, stops sending in it, and commits a Remove of that leaf at once (no ranking delay). If the server refuses the Remove (it believes the device is desired), the warning stays until the leaf goes away; the person can still read, and the UI says that a device RIM cannot verify can read new messages.

Direct messages. A device pins a direct message to its two principals the first time it sees it with exactly two members, one of them its own. It never commits a pending add of any other principal and never counts one as a member when it verifies leaves; while the server lists one (as a member or a pending add), the conversation shows "The server tried to add <name> to this conversation" and the device stops sending there. A DM the server lists with more than two members before the device pinned it gets no group from that device.

Membership lines. "<name> was added" and "<name> was removed" in the timeline come from the Commits the device processed (its own included), never from the server's member list: a principal whose first leaf a Commit added, or whose last leaf it removed, gets a line before the first message of the epoch that Commit made. An incarnation's first Commit adds no lines.

Ranking. Pending changes come from Conversation.mlsGroup. Every group device not in pending removes sorts the ids of the group devices that are not pending removes (lowercase UUID text, ascending) and takes its position as its rank. It waits min(rank, 8) × 15 s plus 0 to 3 s of random jitter, refetches the group, and if a change it can make is still pending and its local epoch equals the server's, it claims key packages and commits all pending changes at once. A device that is about to send an application message commits pending changes first, without waiting (the server refuses its message otherwise, see "Sequencing"). Racing devices are harmless: one Commit wins, the others get STALE_EPOCH.

Key packages #

  • Per device: 20 unclaimed one-time packages and one last-resort package (OpenMLS mark_as_last_resort), each with the lifetime extension not_before = now − 1 h, not_after = now + 90 days − 1 h (so a server whose clock is up to an hour behind the device's still accepts it). Capabilities: suite 0x004F, basic credentials. (OpenMLS checks an added package's lifetime when a device processes the Commit, so a package must outlive the Commit retention a catching-up device may need.)
  • Rotation: on start and every 12 hours while running, a device tops up to 20 when fewer than 10 remain, uploads a new last-resort package when the current one expires within 7 days or was claimed more than 5 times (MlsKeyPackageStatus.lastResortClaims), and deletes private keys of its packages that expired or were replaced more than 7 days ago. Uploading is also the device's MLS-activity signal.
  • Server validation at upload: the bytes decode as an MLSMessage carrying a KeyPackage; the suite is in the server's accepted list ([0x004F]); OpenMLS validation passes (signature, protocol version); the credential is a basic credential whose mls-key statement verifies under the uploading session's device key and names that session's principal and device; the leaf signature key equals mlsKey; not_after is in the future and at most 90 days and 5 minutes away (the 5 minutes absorb a client clock ahead of the server's); the KeyPackageRef (OpenMLS hash_ref, 32 bytes for this suite) is new. A new last-resort package replaces the device's previous one in the same transaction.
  • Claiming: claimMlsKeyPackages takes (principalId, deviceId) pairs for one conversation and returns one package per device, in one transaction: the oldest unclaimed one-time package (FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED), else the last-resort package (not consumed). A package with not_after less than 14 days away is never handed out and counts as absent; a device with no package is left out of the answer, and so is a device whose pending Welcomes leave no room for one more of the largest size ("Welcome delivery and joining"), with its packages left unclaimed. MlsPendingDevice.hasKeyPackage follows the same rules: it is false for a device the claim would leave out, and true again once the device has room. For a re-add target the claim takes the newest one-time package uploaded after the created_at of the device's latest open re-add request, never the last-resort package; with none, VALIDATION (the device has not uploaded key packages since its re-add request). The caller must be a session device that is a group device, or, while the conversation has no group, the session of a conversation member. Every target must be a pending add or a re-add target of that conversation, in the group's suite; when the caller may start a new incarnation ("Incarnations"), the targets are instead the current incarnation's group devices plus pending adds, MLS-active and other than the caller, in the preferred suite, and the claimed packages may be used only by a Commit with incarnation = current + 1, epoch = 0. The claim records (claimed_at, claimed_by_device_key_id, claimed_for_conversation_id, claimed_for_incarnation), and a last-resort row counts its claims in claim_count. A Commit may use a package claimed for its conversation and incarnation by its committer in the last hour; a last-resort row keeps only its latest claim, so a Commit uses it only while that claim is the committer's.
  • Last resort packages are never used for re-adds (see "Lost state").
  • An accepted Commit deletes the one-time packages it added. Claimed one-time rows are deleted 7 days after the claim; expired rows hourly.

Creating a group #

  1. The creator's client calls createConversation(input: { …, encrypted: true }). The server checks that the creator is ready, creates the conversation with encrypted_at = created_at, the members who are not ready invited, and no group. A DM pair that already has a DM gets that conversation back, as today; a plaintext one is then upgraded as in "Migration".
  2. It calls claimMlsKeyPackages for every desired device but its own (with no group there is no mlsGroup to list pending adds: the desired devices are the members' devices with PrincipalDevice.mlsActive, from principalDevices), creates the group at epoch 0 with incarnation 1, adds the devices that got a package, and sends the Commit with the Welcome through submitMlsCommit(incarnation: 1, epoch: 0, cipherSuite: 79).
  3. The server creates mls_group (epoch 1 after the Commit), records the group devices (the committer and the adds), stores the Welcome for each added device, and emits MlsWelcomeAvailable to their users.
  4. If no group exists 60 seconds after creation (the creator crashed), any member device may do step 2; the first accepted Commit wins.

The server refuses an incarnation's first Commit unless every member has at least one device among the resulting group devices (VALIDATION, every member needs at least one device in the group), and unless the committer is MLS-active itself. A conversation's first group (incarnation 1) is created by the device of the member "Migration" names, or, for a conversation created encrypted, by the creator's device during the first 60 seconds and any member's after (FORBIDDEN otherwise).

Welcome delivery and joining #

  • A Welcome is stored once with one recipient row per added device, until the device acknowledges it (ackMlsWelcomes) or for 30 days. myMlsWelcomes returns the session device's pending Welcomes (unacknowledged, unexpired, to conversations its user is a member of), oldest first by (createdAt, id), a page at a time: 100 by default and at most, the most one ackMlsWelcomes takes. after names the last Welcome of the previous page (exclusive); a page shorter than limit is the last. A client that acknowledges every Welcome it read can instead ask for the first page again until it is empty.
  • A device holds at most 256 pending Welcomes and 64 MiB of them. A Commit whose Welcome would take an added device past either is refused (VALIDATION, device … of … has too many pending Welcomes), whole, and nothing is evicted; the claim already leaves such a device out ("Key packages"), so an honest committer meets this only in a race. The device gets room again by acknowledging its Welcomes, as they expire, or when its user leaves their conversations.
  • The device joins (StagedWelcome), checks the group id, verifies every leaf ("Verifying a leaf"), fetches and processes the Commits created in its join epoch and later, then acknowledges. A Welcome it cannot open (the key package's private key is gone) leads to a re-add request.
  • Replayed Welcomes. A device refuses a Welcome when it holds state of the same group (conversation and incarnation) at the Welcome's epoch or later, unless that state waits for a re-add: a Welcome delivered again (Welcomes stay 30 days, and a last-resort key package's private key stays) would roll the group back and let messages of that epoch decrypt again. The refused Welcome is acknowledged. A Welcome that does not open while the device holds working state of its incarnation does not lead to a re-add request either.
  • A new device's history. The feed returns the conversation's older rows too; their epochs are before the device's join epoch (or their incarnation is older), so they cannot be decrypted here. The client shows them as one collapsed line, "Earlier messages were sent before this device joined", with "Restore from history archive" when an archive is set up (history-archive-v1.md); restored rows replace the placeholders. Nothing is ever shown as a decryption failure.

Leaving, removal and the group's end #

  • The server changes membership exactly as today (leaveConversation, removeConversationMember, kick, ban). The member's devices become pending removes; another device commits them.
  • A device that processes its own removal deletes its group state and keeps its plaintext cache until the change feed answers NO_ACCESS, which drops it as for any conversation.
  • Deleting a conversation cascades to its MLS rows. DMs and groups cannot be archived.

Lost state and re-add requests #

A device whose group state is missing or corrupt, that cannot process a Commit, or that is behind past the server's Commit retention, signs a re-add request with its device key over "rim.mls-readd.v1\0" || payload and sends it with requestMlsReadd:

{"v":2,"type":"mls-readd","principalId":"rimp1_…","deviceId":"<uuid>","conversationId":"<uuid>","incarnation":1,"lastEpoch":41,"nonce":"<16 random bytes>","issuedAt":"…"}

lastEpoch is the last epoch it processed, or null with no state. It discards its old group state, sends the request, and only then uploads fresh key packages: a re-add is served from a package uploaded after the request, never from one of the lost state. The server accepts a request only from a device that is still a group device of the incarnation it names, which must be the current one (an older one is STALE_EPOCH), and only when it is fresh:

  • issuedAt is within 10 minutes of the server's clock, either way (VALIDATION);
  • lastEpoch, when not null, is at most the group's epoch and at least the epoch the device was last added at (mls_group_device.added_epoch): state the device holds was reached by joining at that epoch or later, so a request made before its latest add is refused (VALIDATION);
  • its nonce was not used before by that device in that incarnation. The server records every accepted nonce in mls_readd_nonce in the same statement that stores the request, and answers a reused one with CONFLICT (this re-add request was already sent). A device signs a new request, with a new nonce, for every attempt.

Protections against a server forcing re-adds:

Rule Stops
The request is signed by the device key; committers verify it against the device's certificate before acting A server fabricating requests
A re-add uses a one-time key package claimed now, never a last-resort package Forcing reuse of an old last-resort init key that may have leaked
A committer honors at most one request per device and conversation per 24 hours, and the server accepts at most 3 per day Churn and key package draining
The server accepts a request only while it is fresh and only once (issuedAt, lastEpoch, nonce above) Replaying a stored request later in the same incarnation, which would force a Remove and Add and, after 10 minutes without a Commit, open the recovery path to a new incarnation
The re-added leaf is verified like any other Substituting another key

While waiting, the conversation shows "This device can't read new messages until another device re-adds it". A re-add does not restore earlier messages; the cache and the archive do.

Incarnations #

An incarnation is a fresh MLS group for the same conversation (group id with the next incarnation number, epoch 0). submitMlsCommit with incarnation = current + 1 and epoch = 0 is accepted from a group device of the current incarnation only when one holds:

  • Suite migration: cipherSuite is the server's preferred suite and differs from the current incarnation's, and every claimed package offers it;
  • Recovery: the committer has a re-add request on the current incarnation older than 10 minutes and no Commit was accepted on it since that request;
  • No devices left: no group device of the current incarnation is MLS-active for a current member. Then the committer need not be a group device: any MLS-active device of a member may start the next incarnation.

The old mls_group row gets retired_at; every message, Commit and Welcome of it is refused from then on (STALE_EPOCH naming the new incarnation). Clients show "Encryption was reset by <name>'s <device>" at that point in the timeline.

Sequencing #

The server is the MLS delivery service and sequencer. It decodes the outer MLSMessage (protocol version, wire format, and the PrivateMessage header fields group_id, epoch, content_type, authenticated_data) and never decrypts.

One Commit per epoch #

In one transaction:

SELECT epoch FROM mls_group
 WHERE conversation_id = $1 AND incarnation = $2 AND retired_at IS NULL
 FOR UPDATE;
-- epoch must equal the Commit's epoch, else STALE_EPOCH (current epoch and incarnation in the error)
INSERT INTO mls_commit (id, conversation_id, incarnation, epoch, …) VALUES (…);  -- uq_mls_commit_epoch
UPDATE mls_group SET epoch = epoch + 1 WHERE conversation_id = $1 AND incarnation = $2;
-- apply the summary to mls_group_device, consume the Add key packages, store the Welcome

The row lock serializes Commits; the unique index (conversation_id, incarnation, epoch) is the backstop, and a unique violation is also answered STALE_EPOCH. Application messages take the same row FOR SHARE and check the epoch inside their insert transaction. So every application message of epoch e commits before the Commit that ends e, and a client that has seen a Commit already sees every message of the epoch it ended.

Ordering #

  • Application messages are ordered by the message version (the change feed's per-conversation counter, message-change-feed.md), which is epoch-monotone by the locking above. The feed is the only path through which a client decrypts.
  • Commits are ordered by epoch: mlsCommits(conversationId, incarnation, afterEpoch).
  • Client loop, per conversation: fetch new Commits; drain the feed; for each entry, if its epoch is above the local epoch, apply Commits up to it first; decrypt; when the feed is drained, apply the remaining Commits. Commits from realtime events only trigger this loop.
  • Own Commits. The client keeps its Commit pending until the server answers. Accepted: merge. STALE_EPOCH: clear the pending Commit, run the loop, recompute what is still needed, retry. No answer: fetch the Commit at that epoch; if it is the client's own (OwnPendingCommit), merge, else clear and process the other.
  • Own application messages are not decryptable by their sender (OwnPrivateMessage): the sending device stores the plaintext under the message id it chose before sending. On STALE_EPOCH it runs the loop and sends the same content again in the new epoch with the same message id.
  • A Commit the client cannot process (OpenMLS refuses it): the device stops at that epoch, sends a re-add request, and shows the waiting notice. A Commit that processes but whose authenticated-data summary does not name exactly its Add and Remove proposals, or that carries any other proposal (GroupContextExtensions, PSK, ReInit, …), is not merged either: the device stops at that epoch and asks for a re-add the same way, and warns that the committing device misbehaved.

Replay and gaps #

  • MLS deletes each message key after use, so a replayed ciphertext fails to decrypt; the client logs it and ignores it. Application data names the message id, which must equal the row id, so a ciphertext cannot be moved to another row.
  • Per-sender MLS generations skip legitimately (edits replace rows, deletes remove ciphertext), so a generation gap is not an error. Withheld Commits are detected (epochs are consecutive and a later application message names a higher epoch); withheld application messages can only be detected by the feed's cursor contract, not proven. A relay (Stage 5) that withholds them is a residual risk the relay design accepts.

The message envelope #

Rows and tables #

MLS message Stored as
Application message A message row: text null, encrypted true, mls_ciphertext the MLSMessage bytes, mls_incarnation, mls_epoch, sender_device_key_id, franking_commitment, franking_receipt, franking_key_id
Commit An mls_commit row
Welcome An mls_welcome row plus one mls_welcome_recipient row per added device

There is no content-type column on message: only application messages are rows, so the timeline, the change feed and every existing message query keep their meaning. Handshake messages are not timeline items and have their own order (epochs).

Message ids are chosen by the client (UUID v7) for encrypted messages, so the sender can store its plaintext under the id before the server answers. A repeated sendEncryptedMessage with the same id and the same envelope bytes answers the existing message; with other bytes CONFLICT.

Application data #

The MLS application message's plaintext is binary: 0x01 (format version), the 32-byte franking key, then the content: UTF-8 JSON with the statement rules (unknown and duplicate fields refused, every field present, at most 64 KiB):

{"v":1,"type":"message","id":"<uuid>","rev":0,"threadRootId":null,"body":"hi <@rimp1_…>",
 "mentions":["rimp1_…"],"mentionAll":false,"replyTo":null,"forwardedFrom":null,
 "attachments":[],"linkPreviews":[]}
Field Rule
id The message id; must equal the row id and the authenticated data's messageId
rev 0 when sent, +1 per edit; a client never replaces a revision it holds with a lower one
threadRootId The thread root's message id for a thread reply, else null; must match the row's thread (the receiver maps the row's threadId to its root with thread(…).rootMessageId)
body 0 to 10,000 Unicode scalar values; mentions are <@principalId> tokens
mentions The distinct principal ids of the body's <@…> tokens, in order of first appearance, at most 100
mentionAll true when the body notifies everyone: a whole-word @here or @channel outside <@…> tokens, as the server detects it for plaintext
replyTo {"messageId":"<uuid>"} for a timeline reply, else null. Encrypted only: the server's parent_message_id stays null
forwardedFrom {"messageId","conversationId","authorPrincipalId","sentAt"} or null: the forwarder's claim about the origin, shown as "Forwarded by <sender>"
attachments At most 10 entries, see "Attachments"
linkPreviews At most 3 {"url","title","description","siteName"} (strings or null, each at most 2,000 characters), fetched by the sender's client; receivers never fetch

A body, mention list, thread or id that breaks a rule makes the message invalid: the client shows "A message from <sender> could not be verified" and never its content. So does a content whose franking commitment does not match.

Authenticated data #

The PrivateMessage's authenticated_data, readable by the server and signed by the sender's MLS key inside the encryption, is UTF-8 JSON with the same strict rules:

{"v":1,"type":"application","messageId":"<uuid>","franking":"<base64url, 32 bytes>","mentions":["rimp1_…"],"mentionAll":false}
{"v":1,"type":"commit","adds":[{"principalId":"rimp1_…","deviceId":"<uuid>","keyPackageRef":"<base64url>"}],"removes":[{"principalId":"rimp1_…","deviceId":"<uuid>"}]}
  • Application: at most 8 KiB. The server checks messageId against the request (a UUID v7), franking is 32 bytes, every mention is a principal id, distinct, at most 100. The receiver checks after decrypting that messageId, mentions and mentionAll equal the content's and that franking equals the recomputed commitment.
  • Commit: at most 256 KiB, no device added or removed twice. The server checks: every add is a pending add or a re-add target, uses a key package claimed for this conversation by this device in the last hour, named exactly by keyPackageRef, of the group's suite, and belonging to that device; every remove is a group device that is a pending remove or is re-added in the same Commit with a re-add request outstanding; the committer is not removed. A Commit with adds carries a Welcome of the group's suite whose new_member refs are exactly the adds'; one without adds carries none. The receiver checks that the staged Commit's Add proposals have exactly these KeyPackageRefs and its Remove proposals remove exactly these devices, and that it has no other proposal; a Commit that fails this is not merged ("Ordering").

Edits, deletes and metadata #

  • Edit: editEncryptedMessage carries a new application message in the current epoch whose content has the same id, a higher rev, and the complete new message (not a diff: a device that never decrypted the original can render the edit). The server replaces mls_ciphertext, mls_epoch, sender_device_key_id, the franking columns and the mention rows, and records the edit as the next franking revision ("Franking"); the change-feed trigger bumps version and sets edited_at. Only the author (any of their devices) may edit; the receiver checks that the sender's principal is the row author's. An edit counts against the author's message budget (RIM_RATE_MESSAGES_PER_MIN_PER_USER) as a plaintext edit does; resending the envelope that is already stored answers the message without counting.
  • Delete: deleteMessage as today. The server sets is_deleted, nulls mls_ciphertext, and keeps the franking columns. No application message is sent: the server is the authority on deletion (it can drop a row anyway), and a moderator outside a group must be able to delete.
  • Server metadata, visible to the server: reactions (emoji, who, when), pins (which message, who), thread membership (thread_id, the thread rows), the forward origin (forwarded_from_message_id), read states, has_attachments, attachment sizes, sender device, send and edit times, mentions (below). Reactions and pins stay metadata so the server-side counters, events and pinnedMessages keep working; what they leak is the emoji and that someone reacted to or pinned a given message, not the message's content.

Clients: decrypt once, store, render #

  • Sender binding. The row must name its author's principal (author.principalId) and the sending device (encryption.senderDeviceId), and the decrypted MLS sender must be that principal and that device; otherwise the message is "could not be verified", and so is a row without an author principal. The timeline shows a decrypted message as its MLS sender's (device, principal, then the workspace member with that principal), not as the row's author.
  • A message decrypts once per device (MLS deletes each key after use). rim_mls keeps OpenMLS state in the cache database, so decrypting an entry, storing its plaintext row and advancing the ratchet commit in one transaction. The timeline of an encrypted conversation reads from the cache only.
  • Cache rows carry the plaintext as text, the rest as today, plus the envelope's metadata. The upsert rule stays "higher version wins", except that an entry this device cannot decrypt (before its join, older incarnation) is stored only as a placeholder where no plaintext row exists, and never replaces one. RESET on an encrypted conversation keeps plaintext rows (they cannot be refetched) and re-reads the feed over them.
  • An entry more than two epochs behind the device's group (past max_past_epochs) cannot be decrypted either and is a placeholder too. Entries are decrypted in feed order with Commits applied only up to each entry's epoch, so this happens only to a device that processed Commits outside the loop.
  • edited_at and is_deleted come from the feed as for plaintext; a tombstone deletes the plaintext.
  • The archive keeps decrypted text and adds payload.e2ee (status, incarnation, epoch, senderDeviceId, senderPrincipalId, frankingKey, receipt, receiptKeyId, sentAtUs, rev, and content, the exact content bytes a report reveals and the attachment secrets need), an additive field in archive version 1 that S4-3 adds to history-archive-v1.md. Placeholders are not archived. sig stays null. Group state and MLS keys are never archived.

The change feed #

messageChanges is unchanged; encrypted rows come back with text: null and the new Message.encryption field. Encrypted entries are epoch-monotone in version order. An edit appears as one EDITED entry carrying the newest envelope.

Mentions and notifications #

  • The server stores mention rows from the authenticated data: each principal id is mapped to the member with that user.principal_id; ids that are not current members are ignored; the author is skipped. mentionAll is treated as @channel / @here.
  • Notification rules run as today with what the server knows: direct, mention, thread_reply, all and reaction (reactions are not encrypted) work; keyword cannot (the desktop and web clients match keywords after decrypting and show a local notification, without an inbox entry, only where the server sends none: a group at the MENTIONS level, for a message that does not mention the reader; the web client only where the person already allowed the site's notifications). Edits replace mention rows and do not notify again.
  • Digests and any email say "Encrypted message from <name>"; NotificationDue is unchanged.
  • What it leaks: who was mentioned in which message. Accepted, because the server already knows every member, every message's sender and time, and a DM's notifications are per message anyway; without it encrypted conversations would never notify mentions in groups.
  • The server uses the mention list only for mention rows and notification routing. A sender can lie in it, but receivers refuse a message whose list differs from its content, so a lie costs the sender their message.

Attachments #

  • Cipher: AES-256-GCM. Per file a random 32-byte secret; key = HKDF-SHA256(ikm = secret, no salt, info = "rim.e2ee.attachment.v1 key", L = 32), nonce = HKDF-SHA256(ikm = secret, no salt, info = "rim.e2ee.attachment.v1 nonce", L = 12), AAD "rim.e2ee.attachment.v1", one encryption per secret. The blob is the ciphertext with the 16-byte tag appended. AES-GCM rather than XChaCha20-Poly1305 because WebCrypto has it (the web client decrypts up to 25 MB natively and fast) and the desktop already links aes-gcm; with a fresh key per file the nonce cannot repeat.
  • Content entry: {"blobSha256","secret","name","mime","size"}: SHA-256 of the blob, the secret, the file name (1 to 255 characters), the media type as the sender saw it, and the plaintext size. The commitment covers the entry, and the blob hash pins the blob, so one blob decrypts to one file for every member and for a moderator (AES-GCM is not key-committing; the key and the blob are both fixed by the committed content).
  • Upload through the existing flow, after the message is sent: createAttachment(input: { messageId, filename: "encrypted", contentType: "application/octet-stream", sizeBytes, blobSha256 }), the upload to /api/files/upload, finalizeAttachment. In an encrypted conversation blobSha256 is required, the file name and type must be exactly those, and the upload handler accepts application/octet-stream only there and refuses bytes whose SHA-256 or size differ. In a plaintext conversation blobSha256 is refused. The server stores the size and the hash, sniffs nothing, and generates nothing from the bytes.
  • Download: Attachment.blobSha256 matches a content entry; /api/attachments/{id}/download serves application/octet-stream named encrypted.bin; the client checks the hash, decrypts, checks the size, and only then hands the file to the UI with the content entry's name and type.
  • Limits: blob at most MAX_ATTACHMENT_BYTES (25 MiB), so plaintext at most 25 MiB − 16 bytes. No thumbnails in v1: images up to 5 MB are downloaded and shown automatically.

Franking #

A report proves one message's content to the moderators without the server having read it.

  1. Sender: picks a random 32-byte franking key fk per message and edit, and computes the commitment c = HMAC-SHA256(fk, "rim.e2ee.franking.v1\0" || content) over the exact content bytes. fk travels inside the application data; c in the authenticated data.

  2. Server on receipt: stores c and a receipt r = HMAC-SHA256(K, "rim.e2ee.franking-receipt.v1\0" || message id || conversation id || sender user id || sender device id || sent_at_us || c) (ids as 16 raw UUID bytes, the device id being the client device id, sent_at_us the row's sent_at in microseconds as a big-endian i64), where K is the server's current franking key and franking_key_id its id ("Franking keys" below). r and sentAtUs are delivered in Message.encryption. In the same transaction it records the version in message_franking_revision: revision 0 when sent, one more per edit, each with c from the authenticated data, sent_at_us and the client device id of the sending session.

  3. Receiver: recomputes c and refuses the message as invalid on a mismatch, so every message a member can see is reportable. fk must be exactly 32 bytes (HMAC's key padding would otherwise let two keys collide). It keeps fk, r and sentAtUs with the plaintext.

  4. Report (reportEncryptedMessage): the reporter sends the content bytes, fk, the receipt, its key id, the sender ids and sentAtUs. The server recomputes c from the content and fk and checks, comparing secrets and MACs in constant time:

    • the sender user is the row's author, and c, sentAtUs and the sender device equal those of any recorded revision of the message (or the row's current franking_commitment, sent_at and sender device, while the row still names one). Every revision is compared, without stopping at the first match;
    • r recomputed with key receiptKeyId (any key in the key file, "Franking keys") equals the receipt.

    The revision check means a holder of K (a stolen key file or backup) cannot have the server verify content the sender never sent: the receipt alone proves only that K signed it. An edit adds a revision rather than replacing one, so every version a receiver saw stays reportable: a sender who edits an abusive message does not escape a report of what was shown. A message the sender deleted keeps its row and revisions (is_deleted, mls_ciphertext nulled), and no retention job deletes message rows, so it stays reportable; the report and the revisions reference the row and go with it only when its conversation or workspace is deleted. A valid report stores the content in message_report (franked_content, franking_verified_at); an invalid one is refused (VALIDATION, the report does not match the message). The usual report rules hold (one per reporter and message).

  5. Moderator view (S4-5): the body as plain text with mentions resolved, the attachment names and sizes (a moderator's client downloads a reported attachment by its blobSha256 through a report-scoped route and decrypts it with the secret from the verified content), sender, device, conversation and time, and the line "Verified: this is the exact message <sender>'s device sent. Other messages in the conversation are not shown and are not verified." A verified report lets a moderator delete that message even outside the conversation.

    • The report stores the sender's device id the receipt covered (message_report.franked_sender_device_id). messageReports shows every moderator a verified report's messageAuthorId, messageSentAt, frankingVerifiedAt, frankedSenderDeviceId and frankedContentExpiresAt, never its content; messageReport(serverId, reportId) (Owner/Moderator) adds frankedContent and records e2ee.report_viewed.
    • GET /api/reports/{reportId}/attachments/{blobSha256} (session; Owner/Moderator of the report's workspace) serves the blob of an attachment of the reported message, or a stored blob of the workspace that the verified content names, as application/octet-stream named encrypted.bin with X-Content-Type-Options: nosniff; anything else is a bare 404.
    • deleteMessage by an Owner/Moderator who cannot read the conversation succeeds when the message has a verified report.
    • In the direct space, where everyone is a guest, the instance operators take the moderators' place for reports: they list, open and resolve them, download their attachments and delete a message with a verified report, and nobody else in the direct space can (docs/messaging.md, "Reports"). A report there that shows the author also carries their principal id, messageAuthorPrincipalId.

Franking keys. K lives in the franking key file, RIM_FRANKING_KEY_FILE (default <RIM_DATA_DIR>/franking-key.json, mode 0600; the server refuses a file the group or others can read):

{"v":1,"keys":[{"id":1,"key":"<base64url, 32 bytes>","createdAt":"2026-10-10T00:00:00Z"}]}
  • The key with the highest id is current: it signs every new receipt and its id is the message's franking_key_id. Keys are held in memory in zeroizing buffers.
  • At first boot without the file the server creates it with one random key, id 1. It refuses to start when the file is missing but messages carry a franking_key_id (a lost file: restore it). message.franking_key_id references no table (migration drop_message_franking_key_fk); the franking_key table the first migration created is dropped (migration drop_franking_key).
  • rim_server franking-key rotate adds a key with the next id, which becomes current. Every key stays in the file and keeps verifying the receipts it signed for good: a receipt is never signed again, so a message stays reportable for as long as it exists. What stops a forged report is the check against the stored revisions, not a key's age. rim_server franking-key show lists the keys. A running server reads the file again every minute, and at once (at most every ten seconds) when a report names a key id above the ones it holds.

Limits. A report proves one message, not its context: what it replied to, what came before, or whether the reporter provoked it (a reporter may report several messages, each proven alone). The revealed content is stored on the server from then on. Franking adds no deniability loss: MLS messages are already signed by the sender's MLS key, so any member holding a plaintext and its signature could convince a third party. Message deniability is deferred.

Plugins and the client message service #

S4-4 implements the client message service; this is its contract.

  • Methods (plugin to host, routed by both hosts but not frozen in protocol v1, listed under x-notFrozen in rim-ui's protocol/plugin-protocol-v1.schema.json; reference in rim-ui docs/plugin-chat-methods.md): rim.chat.list_messages { conversationId, before?, limit? } → { messages, hasOlder } (oldest first, before a message id, limit 1 to 100, default 50); rim.chat.get_message { messageId } → { message } (null when not on this device); rim.chat.search_messages { query, conversationId?, limit? } → { messages } (local search, newest first); rim.chat.post_message { conversationId, body, threadId? } → { id }. They replace the earlier rim.chat.get_messages and rim.chat.send_message names.
  • Capabilities. chat.read and chat.write cover plaintext conversations. chat.read.encrypted and chat.write.encrypted cover encrypted ones and require the plain capability as well (a manifest asking for an encrypted one without the plain one is refused at install; the broker checks the plain one per method, the service the encrypted one per conversation, both answering CAPABILITY_DENIED). Each is its own line in the consent prompt; chat.read.encrypted reads "Read your encrypted DMs and groups: this plugin sees their decrypted text and can send it anywhere its other permissions allow.", and chat.write.encrypted "Post as you in your encrypted DMs and groups: this plugin sends messages in your name there, encrypted like your own." Web and desktop plugins get the same names. A conversation counts as encrypted for its old plaintext too.
  • What the service returns: { id, conversationId, threadId, author: { principalId, displayName }, text, sentAt, editedAt, encrypted, attachments: [{ name, mime, size }] }, read from the local cache, with author a protocol Principal and <@user> mentions as principal ids. Never franking keys, attachment secrets or blob hashes, MLS state, ciphertext or keys of any kind. Placeholders, messages that could not be verified, tombstones and unacknowledged sends are left out. Plugins cannot fetch attachment bytes in v1. A conversation not fully cached on the device answers INTERNAL_ERROR with data.reason: "notCached"; the web app, whose plugin service does not yet read the browser's encrypted store, answers reads with "noCache".
  • Writes go through the same send path as the person's own messages and are encrypted by the client.
  • Audit: the host records, per plugin, when each grant was given and revoked and a daily count of encrypted messages read, shown in the plugin's settings. Revoking stops the plugin, as for every capability.

Migration of existing conversations #

  • Who upgrades: in a DM either member's device; in a group a device of the creator (created_by), or of any member when the creator is null or no longer a member. The device must be a desktop device: the web client takes part in encrypted conversations and creates new ones, but never upgrades an existing one. A DM or group whose eligible members open it only in browsers stays plaintext until one of them opens it on a desktop.
  • When: the eligible device upgrades automatically when it opens the conversation and every member is ready. While someone is not, the conversation shows "Not encrypted yet: <name> has not set up encryption on a desktop device".
  • How: as "Creating a group" step 2 on the existing conversation. The server sets encrypted_at in the transaction that accepts incarnation 1's first Commit, and emits ConversationUpdated.
  • The marker is Conversation.encryptedAt: clients render "Messages from here on are end-to-end encrypted" at that point, and a quiet "sent before encryption" note on older messages. No message row is created.
  • Old plaintext stays on the server, readable by members as before (members added later can still read it). People can delete their own old messages.

The web client (S4-6) #

  • The same rim_mls crate in wasm with the libcrux provider's portable code. OpenMLS's storage trait is synchronous and IndexedDB is not, so the crate keeps state in memory and writes the entries an operation changed to IndexedDB, in one transaction with the plaintext rows and records the operation produced, before the operation's result is used (no ciphertext is sent, and no plaintext shown, before the write lands). A failed write rolls the in-memory state back, so a message is still decrypted once.
  • Plaintext store: the web app has no general message cache; decrypt-once requires one, so the web client keeps an IndexedDB store of decrypted messages for encrypted conversations.
  • Keys: the MLS signature key and HPKE private keys are raw bytes in IndexedDB, because OpenMLS signs and decapsulates synchronously; the device key stays a non-extractable WebCrypto key and signs only mls-key statements and re-add requests. There is no keyring.
  • Key packages are refreshed only while a tab is open. A browser unused for 60 days leaves the groups and is re-added when it comes back: before uploading key packages it sends a re-add request where the server still counts it as a group device and drops its group state elsewhere, then joins from the Welcomes its pending adds bring. What was sent meanwhile is not readable there.
  • One tab of a browser runs encryption for an account (a lock held while the page lives, the Web Locks API); another tab says so and waits. Two tabs would advance two copies of the state.
  • No migration from the web client ("Migration"), and no link previews ("Server features in encrypted conversations").
  • The web app's content security policy allows 'wasm-unsafe-eval' in script-src to compile the module.
  • The weaker-storage notice of web-linked-device.md is extended: "Encrypted conversations in this browser are only as safe as this browser profile."

The franking_key table above is no longer used ("Franking keys"). A second migration, add_report_sender_device, adds message_report.franked_sender_device_id uuid NULL. Retention: Commits 90 days, Welcomes 30 days, claimed key packages 7 days, re-add requests once fulfilled or after 7 days, re-add nonces 1 day (longer than a request stays fresh), franked_content 90 days after the report is resolved.

NewMessage and MessageUpdated carry message.encryption (the MessageEncryption fields, snake case) and text: null. New variants, delivered to the conversation's members (server-side scope lists as today):

epoch is the Commit's epoch. Threat model #

Adversary Can Cannot
The server (full database and code) See metadata: members, devices, timing, sizes, sender devices, mentions, reactions, pins, threads, forward origins; withhold or delay messages and Commits; refuse Commits; delete rows; ask nothing a device would sign Read content or attachments; add a device of its own (it holds no device key to sign mls-key); forge a message; move a ciphertext to another row or conversation; force a last-resort re-add; read reports it did not receive
A relay (Stage 5) Less than the server: it sees ciphertext and its own users' metadata, and can withhold Everything the server cannot
A compromised current device Read everything its leaf can read, past messages in its cache and archive, until it is revoked and removed; send as that device Read after its removal (forward secrecy of later epochs); recover messages whose keys were used and deleted, other than through its cache
A revoked device Read messages until its removal Commit is accepted (the server refuses others' messages while the removal is pending, so a sender commits it first) Read anything after that Commit
A member's old, unrevoked device Read like any device; it is removed after 60 days without a key package upload —
A former member Keep what its devices decrypted Read anything after its devices' removal
The drive provider See archive files and sizes (history-archive-v1.md) Read them; group state is never there

Post-compromise security comes from Commits with update paths (every device at most weekly, and every membership Commit). Quantum: key agreement is hybrid (X-Wing); signatures and authentication are classical.
